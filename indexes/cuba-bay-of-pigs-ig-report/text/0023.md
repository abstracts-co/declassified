Views of Joint Chiefs
30. Agency participants in the project have sought to
defend the invasion plan by citing the approval given to the
plan by the Joint Chiefs of Staff (JCS). To this argument,
members of the JCS have replied, in the course of another
inquiry, (1) that the final plan was presented to them only
orally, which prevented normal staffing; (2) that they regarded
the operation as being solely CIA's, with the military called
on to furnish various types of support and the chie? interest
of the JCS being to see to it that every kind of support
requested was furnished; (3) that they went on the assumption
that full air support would be furnished and control of the
air secured and on the Agency's assurances that a great number
of insurgents would immediately join forces with the invasion
forces; and (4) that, in the event the battle went against then,
the brigade would at once "go guerrilla" and take to the hills.
