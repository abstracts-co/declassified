Preparations Endorsed
63. Such a meeting was held 22 January, and the project and
current preparations were generally endorsed. At a meeting with
the new President on 28 January the Agency was authorized to
continue present activities and was instructed to submit the
tactical paramilitary plan to the Joint Chiefs of Staff for
analysis. Shortly thereafter, in an attempt to get a high-level
internal review of the plan, it was briefed to Gen. Cabell,
Gen. Bull (consultant) and Adm. Wright (ONE). By 6 February
the Joint Chiefs had returned a favorable evaluation of the
strike plan, together with a number of suggestions.

64. On 17 February the Agency presented a paper (Annex B)
to the President which outlined three possible courses of action
against Castro.
65. Noting plans for early formation of a government in
exile, the paper described the growing strength of the Castro
regime under Bloc Support and observed: "Therefore » after
some date probably no more than six months away it will become

The option of action by the Cuban opposition will no longer be

66. This paper found the use of small-scale guerrilla
groups not feasible and advocated a surprise landing of a mili-
tary force, concluding that the brigade had a good chance of
overthrowing Castro “or at the very least causing a damaging
civil war without requiring the U.S. ‘to commit itself to overt
action against Cuba."
67. Following presentation of this paper to the President,

at least two weeks before a decision would be made as to use
of the invasion force. _ They thereupon withheld action to expand
the force up to 1,000 for the time being.
